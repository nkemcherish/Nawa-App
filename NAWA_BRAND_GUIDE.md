# Nawa Brand Guide

## Brand essence
**Nawa** helps people make sense of their money. It should feel like a calm, sharp friend who can explain a confusing bank statement without judgement.

**Tagline:** Your money, explained.

## Personality
- Calm, not cold
- Clear, not simplistic
- Trustworthy, not corporate
- Human, not overly playful
- Helpful, never judgemental

## Visual system
| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Forest | `#173D32` | Primary brand colour, main buttons, strong emphasis |
| Warm Paper | `#F7F4EC` | Main background |
| Sage | `#C7DB9A` | Selected states, subtle highlights, positive accents |
| Ink | `#292D29` | Primary text |
| Mist | `#E7E4DC` | Borders, dividers, quiet surfaces |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | High-contrast surfaces when needed |
| Muted text | `#666B65` | Secondary text; verify contrast on each background |

## Typography
- Display/headlines: DM Serif Display, with a system serif fallback.
- Interface/body: Inter, with a system sans-serif fallback.
- Use headlines sparingly. Numbers should be legible and easy to scan.
- Avoid tiny labels, overly tight line-height, and long all-caps headings.

## Logo
Use `assets/nawa-logo.svg`. The mark uses a circular coin-like form with a small intentional break to suggest money being examined and understood, paired with a wordmark. Keep the SVG scalable and crisp. Do not stretch it, add shadows, or place it over busy imagery. Use the standalone mark for small app-icon explorations and the full lockup for welcome/home branding.

## Layout
- Mobile first.
- Comfortable horizontal padding and safe-area spacing.
- Use 12–16 px corner radii consistently, not on every element by default.
- Prefer clear sections and whitespace over stacks of cards.
- Use charts only when they help explain a real pattern.
- Make primary actions obvious and secondary actions quiet.
- All interactive controls need a clear pressed, selected, disabled, loading, or error state where relevant.

## Do
- Explain financial language in plain English.
- Show how totals are defined.
- Label uncertainty and ask the user to review ambiguous transactions.
- Keep the interface calm, warm, and legible.
- Use real calculated values, not hand-entered summary totals.

## Avoid
- Neon gradients, excessive glassmorphism, glossy 3D coins, and stock-market clichés.
- Judgemental copy such as “You wasted money.”
- Generic dashboard grids full of tiny metrics.
- Unexplained financial jargon.
- Fake processing, fake results, or claims that an unreadable file was analysed.

## Suggested voice
- “Let’s look at your month.”
- “This transfer may be between your own accounts. Check it before we count it.”
- “We couldn’t read this statement format yet. You can try a CSV export or explore the demo.”
- “Your report is saved on this device.”

Avoid shame, alarmist language, and claims that Nawa can predict or guarantee financial outcomes.
